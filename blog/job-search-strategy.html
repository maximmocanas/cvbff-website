Job Search Strategy: How to Apply for 20 Jobs in 2 Hours (Without Spamming)

May 2026 · 8 min read

Most active job seekers fall into one of two failure modes. The first is the perfectionist: spends three hours rewriting their CV for a single application, sends 4 applications a week, and burns out after a month with nothing to show. The second is the spammer: sends the same generic CV to 200 jobs a week, gets near-zero responses, and concludes that the market is broken.

Both are wrong. The real workflow is a middle path: batch-apply to 20 carefully chosen roles in a 2-hour session, with each application properly tailored. This guide shows you how. It assumes you've already done the foundational work of writing a good CV; if you haven't, start with our CV tailoring guide first.

Why Batching Works

The reason 20 applications in 2 hours is realistic is that most of the work in a job application is overhead: opening tabs, copying job descriptions, logging into different sites, filling in name and email for the hundredth time, exporting PDFs. The actual decision-making and writing is a small fraction.

When you batch, you do all the overhead in one go and your focus stays on the work that matters. Context-switching kills productivity. Doing one application Monday, one Tuesday, one Friday means re-loading the same mental state three times.

Job searching is a workflow problem disguised as a writing problem. The people who get the most interviews aren't the best writers; they're the most efficient operators.

Before You Start: One-Time Setup (30 Minutes, Once)

This is the work you do once, then never again. Skipping this is what makes job hunting feel exhausting.

1. Build a master CV profile

Write out everything: every role, every project, every achievement, with numbers. Don't worry about length, you'll cut this down for individual applications. You're building a library to draw from.

2. Write three CV variants

If you're targeting more than one type of role (say, product manager and product marketing), prepare a base CV for each. You'll still tailor per application, but starting from the closer base saves time.

3. Write a reusable cover letter spine

Not a template you'll paste verbatim, a structure. Opening line (you'll customise per application), middle paragraph (your strongest 2 proof points, lightly adjusted), close (always the same).

4. Set up a simple tracker

A Google Sheet or Notion table with columns: Company, Role, Date Applied, Source, Tailored?, Response, Notes. Don't skip this. Knowing what you've applied to in week 4 of your search is impossible without one.

The 2-Hour Application Session

With the setup done, here's the actual session, broken into time-boxed phases.

Phase 1: Triage (15 minutes)

Open one tab per job board you use (LinkedIn, Indeed, Wellfound, Welcome to the Jungle, plus any niche boards for your industry). Scroll through new postings from the last 7 days. For each one, ask yourself one question: "Could I see myself doing this job?"

If yes, open it in a new tab. If no, skip immediately. Don't read the full posting yet. Don't second-guess salary or location yet. You're just casting a wide net.

Aim for 30 to 40 tabs at the end of triage. You're going to cut this in half in the next phase.

Phase 2: Score and Cut (15 minutes)

Now read each posting properly. Score each one quickly:

  • A: Strong match, you genuinely want this role. Apply with full effort.
  • B: Decent match. Worth applying with standard effort.
  • C: Weak match or red flags (massive scope creep, vague title, dodgy company). Skip.

Close the C tabs immediately. You should end with about 15 to 25 A/B roles. That's your application list for the session.

This phase is where most job seekers waste time agonising. Don't. If a job is borderline, treat it as a B and move on. The cost of applying to a slightly-wrong job is 5 minutes. The cost of not applying to a great job is unknowable.

Phase 3: Batch Apply (75 minutes)

Now you apply. Set a timer: 4 minutes per A-roles, 2 minutes per B-roles. That's it. Stick to the timer.

For each application:

  1. Generate a tailored CV against the posting (manually, this is the slow step; with a tool, 30 seconds)
  2. Generate a tailored cover letter (same)
  3. Customise the opening line of the cover letter to mention something specific to the company
  4. Submit through the application form
  5. Log it in your tracker (one row, takes 10 seconds)
  6. Close the tab

The customised opening line is the one bit you cannot automate. It's the difference between an application that looks tailored and one that is tailored. Don't skip it.

Phase 4: Follow-Up Setup (15 minutes)

For each A-role you applied to, find the hiring manager or recruiter on LinkedIn and add them with a short connection note: "Hi [name], just applied for the [role] at [company]. Would love to connect."

This single move dramatically increases your response rate. Most applications go into a black box. A LinkedIn connection puts a name and face in the recruiter's inbox.

How to Hit 20 Applications Without Burning Out

The 2-hour session is intense. Don't do it every day. A realistic cadence:

  • Monday morning: 2-hour session, target 20 applications
  • Wednesday morning: 1-hour mini-session, target 10 applications
  • Friday morning: 1-hour mini-session, target 10 applications + follow up on Monday's applications

That's 40 quality applications per week. Most active job seekers send 5 to 10 per week, almost all of them generic. 40 tailored applications puts you in the top 1% of the funnel.

What to Track, What to Ignore

Track:

  • Applications sent per week
  • Response rate (any response, positive or negative)
  • Interview rate (first-round invitations)
  • Source of each response (which job board or channel converts best)

Ignore:

  • Salary on every posting (until later, most are negotiable)
  • How long ago the role was posted (within reason; old roles still hire)
  • Whether you're "perfectly qualified" (you don't need to tick every box)
  • What the response rate of other job seekers is (everyone lies on Twitter)

Realistic Numbers

If your CV is solid and you're applying to roles in your actual range, here's what to expect from a focused 40-applications-per-week schedule:

  • Response rate: 15 to 25%, so 6 to 10 responses per week
  • First-round interview rate: 5 to 10%, so 2 to 4 first-rounds per week
  • Offer timeline: 6 to 12 weeks from starting a focused search to first offer

If you're getting numbers below this for several weeks, the problem isn't the workflow, it's something specific about the CV or the role-targeting. Common CV mistakes are the first thing to check.
